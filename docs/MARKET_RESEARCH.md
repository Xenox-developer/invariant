## 1. Decision brief

**The strongest documented problem is getting trustworthy, reviewable results from agent work.** Isolation and persistence are useful foundations, but established alternatives already supply them. Our next investment should test whether INVARIANT can remove the work between an agent saying it finished and another engineer being able to verify the result. **Confidence: medium in the problem; low in demand for our particular solution.** This is analyst synthesis, not a population estimate. (C-026–C-028; underlying evidence below.)

**Conditional first customer:** an engineering lead at a product team of 10–50 developers using at least two coding-agent products. The proposed job is: *“Give a teammate an agent-made change they can reproduce and review, with the actual checks and access boundaries recorded.”* The team size and multi-agent criterion are recruitment hypotheses, not discovered market boundaries. (C-029; A-001, A-002.)

**Recommendation:** run a comparative pilot for reproducible verification and handoff before expanding the environment product. Keep local persistence as a foundation. Test recovery and plain-language permissions as supporting capabilities; do not assume either is the reason customers will buy. Existing CI, native agent controls and Docker Sandboxes must be explicit comparison options.

**Do not claim that the industry needs another sandbox.** This research identifies a plausible entry point, not an uncontested category or confirmed willingness to pay. A negative pilot is a reason to stop expansion.

## 7. Recommended experiment and minimum useful product

**Proposed promise:** *“Your agent's change arrives with a reproducible environment and independently recorded checks.”* This is a product hypothesis, not an available feature.

**Recruitment target:** teams of 10–50 developers already using two or more coding agents, with a recent example of review delay caused by missing execution evidence or environment setup. Interview reviewers and the budget owner separately. Exclude organizations seeking only a better model, a generic chat interface or production system access. (C-029.)

**Workflow to test:** a developer gives an approved agent a task in an isolated project copy; the environment preserves needed tools; a separate supervisor executes team-approved checks against the resulting change; the reviewer receives the patch, exact commands/results, environment identity, policy identity and a clear list of unchecked behavior; a teammate reproduces the checks. The customer retains their normal review and merge process.

**Minimum pilot capability:** support Linux-compatible repositories with executable build/test checks; reuse existing devcontainer definitions where practical; cover Codex plus one second agent selected from pilot users' existing tools. Never claim interchangeable native chat histories. Scope network access explicitly. Record outcomes independently of the model's completion claim, preserve failed/interrupted states, and provide machine-readable evidence alongside a short human report. Exported environments and artifacts must exclude secrets.

**Important implementation distinction:** checks run under an independently controlled definition/reference, while the generated patch is disclosed for review. If an agent changes tests, verification configuration or dependencies, show it and do not silently treat its altered checks as equivalent to the approved baseline. A passing test is limited evidence, not proof of correctness or human intent. An environment hash identifies state; it does not make an unreviewed environment trustworthy.

**Scope limits:** no arbitrary production API access, host credential exposure, compliance certification, nested-Docker support or universal technology compatibility claim. Browser/service-heavy applications that exceed the backend's capabilities must be disclosed and excluded from the initial pilot rather than weakening isolation. Recovery must say which local files/state can be restored and which external actions cannot.

**Differentiation to test:** less reviewer setup and uncertainty than the team's existing CI/native-agent workflow, across its current agents, without changing model subscriptions. This is not established exclusivity. If Docker plus CI already does this with comparable effort, reuse those components or abandon the extra product.

**Pricing hypothesis:** after value is observed, offer **$200 per team/month for up to ten active users**, excluding model/compute charges. This is an experimental offer, not measured willingness to pay or a pricing forecast. Record refusals and their stated alternatives. Do not infer acceptance from enthusiasm or a free trial. (C-030; A-003.)

## 8. Interview and pilot protocol

These are proposed research activities. No recruitment, messages, customer access or billing has been executed. Counts and thresholds below are chosen decision rules, not statistical power calculations. (C-031; A-004.)

### Interviews: 12 people across six organizations

Recruit four developers/reviewers, four engineering leads and four platform/security owners. Include organizations satisfied with native controls or Docker/CI, and people who stopped using agents. At least half of organizations should be outside the founder's immediate network. Obtain consent, retain role/organization-size categories rather than names in analysis, and request no proprietary code or credentials.

Ask about behavior before showing the concept:

1. Describe the last agent-made change you reviewed or handed off. What happened between completion and acceptance?
2. Which checks actually ran, and how did you know? Could someone else reproduce them?
3. Show a sanitized example of the delay, failed setup, permission interruption or recovery problem. When did it last recur?
4. What did you try—native settings, Git, CI, devcontainers, another provider? Why was it insufficient, or why was it enough?
5. Who did the extra work, approximately how long did it take, and what was delayed? Separate recollection from recorded time.
6. What tools are already paid for? Who approves another purchase, and which budget would fund it?
7. What restrictions would block installing an execution wrapper? What data may leave the machine or reach a model?
8. Which tool would you stop paying for or which work would disappear if this succeeded?

Only then show the workflow and proposed price. Ask for a real repository pilot and a budget-owner conversation, not a hypothetical willingness-to-pay rating. Log disconfirming responses and current-tool satisfaction.

**Interview gate:** proceed to a pilot only if at least four of six organizations supply a concrete problem recurring at least weekly, at least three identify a budget owner and commit a real repository, and the recurring problem is substantially addressable through execution/verification rather than model quality. If the leading pain is entirely different, revise the hypothesis before building.

### Comparative pilot: three teams, two baseline weeks and two pilot weeks

Use real tasks of similar type/size. Each team contributes at least five baseline handoffs and five pilot handoffs. Match task type and estimated size prospectively across phases; record task selection, agent/model changes and other differences. This small convenience pilot cannot support an industry-wide causal productivity claim.

Before work begins, record the existing native-agent/CI/Docker workflow, approved check definitions, access policy, repository class, model/agent versions and baseline compute costs. Teams with an adequate incumbent remain in the comparison. Do not make the baseline artificially worse.

Measure independently:

- Human minutes spent setting up, reproducing and validating each handoff; exclude queue time and waiting unless separately reported.
- Teammate reproduction success from the supplied state, without undocumented manual repair.
- Actual check execution versus claimed success; record pass, fail, skipped, blocked and interrupted separately.
- Policy interruptions and whether the user understood what was blocked and how to recover.
- Regressions, credential/export problems, escape from the agreed project scope, and added support/compute costs.
- Whether the team chooses to continue using the workflow when optional and whether its budget owner pays.

**Proceed only if all gates pass:** at least two teams reduce median human setup/reproduction/validation minutes by 30% against their own baseline; at least 90% of pilot handoffs reproduce the declared checks; no false-success reports in the agreed failure probes; no critical boundary or secret-export failure; at least two teams pay $200 for a subsequent month; and support averages no more than one hour per team per week. Report each team's results and task mix, not only pooled averages. These are product thresholds, not confidence intervals or promises.

**Stop or revise** if CI/native controls meet the need with similar effort, the payment gate fails, value depends mainly on custom consulting, users require incompatible application backends, or our wrapper disrupts preferred agent workflows. Any critical access/secret failure stops that pilot immediately. Do not broaden into an enterprise platform to avoid a failed test of the initial thesis.

## 9. Methods, assumptions and evidence audit

**Market boundary:** developer-facing coding-agent execution and adjacent verification, recovery and control workflows. Users, engineering/platform buyers and security buyers are distinct. Global English-language public evidence is used for discovery, not geographic demand sizing. Revenue, TAM/SAM/SOM, market shares and forecasts were not estimated.

**Coverage:** 50 ledger records: 15 issue threads, 3 local implementation records, one methodology attribution and 31 other public research/product records. Sources were selected for decision relevance, not exhaustiveness. Three survey programs were examined: Stack Overflow, GitLab/Harris and Sonar; their methodological transparency differs. The local reports establish product fit only, and the methodology citation supplies no demand evidence.

**Survey safeguards:** Stack Overflow's [methodology](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2026/methodology) reports 30,903 valid responses across 169 countries via community recruitment. Item bases and wording differ. It is not a probability sample of the industry; no representative weighting was established. Sonar screens for prior AI use. GitLab's reviewed release does not disclose full sampling details. No percentages were pooled, no trend was computed and no budget-intention figure was converted into sales. (C-002.)

**Source hierarchy and limits:** official product pages establish vendor-described features and offers, not actual performance or demand. Original studies and first-party deployment accounts establish narrower observations. Tracker reports are self-reports and were not reproduced here. Current official pricing was preferred over third-party articles. Living pages have no guaranteed historical snapshot; publication dates are recorded as not stated where unavailable. Conflicting documentation and uncertain resolution remain visible. No paid estimate, funding round, user-count marketing claim or GitHub-star total was treated as market demand.

**Evidence files:** [sources.csv](/Users/nikchin/Desktop/invariant/docs/market-research/sources.csv) records source IDs, dates, methods, populations, limitations, billing units, issue API provenance and local-file hashes. [claims.csv](/Users/nikchin/Desktop/invariant/docs/market-research/claims.csv) maps every numbered factual claim and the principal inferences/recommendations to sources. Local records use `local:` project-relative references rather than invented web URLs; their hashes bind the assessment to files read. Public full texts and usernames were not copied into these deliverables.

**Validation performed:** the bundled evidence validator accepted the 47 public source records; a separate path/hash/date check verified the three local records. The claim auditor accepted all 47 claims against all 50 source IDs with no missing or unused sources. Missing publication-date warnings for living pages were retained. These checks establish structural traceability, not the truth of every source or a validated market.

**Explicit assumptions:** A-001: an early product should be deliverable by a small team using the existing Linux environment foundation. A-002: mixed-agent small engineering teams are reachable and may have a distinct handoff problem; unvalidated. A-003: $200/team/month is worth testing; no demand or margin validation. A-004: the interview/pilot thresholds are chosen go/no-go rules; they are not representative-study inference. These assumptions must change if the observed buyer behavior contradicts them.
